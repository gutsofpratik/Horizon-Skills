# Industry-Aware Pricing: Construction, AEC, and Facilities

Use alongside the matching pricing workflow after the economic model clearly resolves to this primary lane.

## Separate the subtypes

### Construction/public works

A typical estimate may include:

`quantity takeoff by work item/trade`
`x labor/material/equipment/subcontract unit cost`
`+ general conditions and supervision`
`+ mobilization/demobilization and site logistics`
`+ temporary controls, testing, safety, quality, and closeout`
`+ bonds, insurance, permits, taxes, and fees when applicable`
`+ escalation and schedule impacts`
`+ contingency/risk reserve`
`+ home-office indirects and fee`

GovTribe cannot produce a defensible takeoff or live local trade estimate without drawings, quantities, productivity, quotes/cost data, and company assumptions.

### Architect-engineer/licensed design

A-E pricing is not construction pricing. Model:

- work breakdown and deliverables
- discipline/labor mix, hours, and rates
- subconsultants and specialty services
- surveys, testing, travel, printing/data, and other direct costs
- design reviews, QA/QC, meetings, permitting support, construction-phase services, and revisions
- indirect rates and fee according to company practice and procurement rules

For federal A-E, qualifications selection may precede fee negotiation. Do not use low-price construction comparables as the selection model.

### Design-build

Keep design, preconstruction, construction, commissioning, and design-management risk visible. Reconcile design assumptions with construction scope, allowances, design-to-budget, and schedule.

### Facilities O&M/MRO

Model:

- asset inventory, preventive maintenance, corrective work orders, response time, staffing/shifts, CMMS, tools, vehicles, consumables, materials, subcontracted trades, emergencies, backlog, transition, and lifecycle replacement

Separate included materials from reimbursable/pass-through materials and project work.

### Energy/utilities/performance projects

Model:

- baseline, audit/design, equipment, installation, commissioning, financing, utility incentives, measurement and verification, O&M, savings guarantee, degradation, tariff, escalation, and performance shortfall risk

Do not infer savings or guarantee economics without source data and engineering inputs.

## Source-confirmed inputs to extract

- drawings/specifications, quantities, bid schedule/UOM, alternates, allowances, owner-furnished items, and options
- site, geography, occupied conditions, access, phasing, shutdowns, schedule, weather, long-lead, and security
- wage determination/classifications, union/PLA, apprenticeship, local hire, and certified payroll when applicable
- safety, quality, testing, commissioning, submittals, closeout, warranty, and liquidated damages
- bonds, insurance, retainage, payment terms, permits, taxes, licenses, and prequalification
- subcontractor/trade/self-performance/workshare requirements
- A-E disciplines, deliverables, reviews, site visits, construction-phase services, and fee instructions
- facilities asset/work-order/service-level/material and transition data

## Evidence use

- Use state/local line items only when units, quantities, trade/project type, geography, date, parent context, and included scope align.
- Federal award value can support project magnitude, buyer budget, and competitor context but not unit cost.
- BLS can orient trade/professional wages but is not a project wage determination or productivity source.
- GSA rates may inform professional/A-E or facilities support labor where relevant, not construction trade/unit pricing.
- SCI may support labor footprint for federal facilities O&M, not construction project cost.
- Current subcontractor/vendor quotes and quantity takeoffs are primary estimating inputs when available.

## Normalize construction comparables

At minimum, compare:

- facility/asset and project type
- new construction vs renovation/repair/remediation
- gross area/quantity and trade mix
- geography and local labor/material market
- secure/occupied/remote/hazardous conditions
- delivery method and contract type
- wage regime and date
- schedule/phasing/long-lead
- owner-furnished scope, alternates, allowances, and included design
- escalation and market conditions

Price per square foot is only useful when facility type, scope, quality, site, and date are close enough. Do not use it as a universal estimator.

## Construction risk scenarios

Test:

- material/subcontract quote movement
- wage/productivity
- quantities and design maturity
- unknown conditions/hazardous materials
- long-lead and schedule compression
- occupied-site/phasing/outages
- weather/site access
- bonds/insurance/cash flow/retainage
- permits/utility coordination
- escalation and option timing
- contingency and change exposure

## A-E scenarios

Test:

- discipline hours and design iterations
- subconsultant scope
- surveys/investigations and owner data quality
- meetings/reviews/permit cycles
- construction-phase services and duration
- redesign/rework assumptions
- fee/indirect treatment and negotiation basis

## State/local considerations

- Verify current prevailing wage, certified payroll, public-works taxes, bid alternates, retainage, bond requirements, apprenticeship/local hire, price adjustment, and local preference.
- JOC or unit-price books may use jurisdiction-specific coefficients and task catalogs not present in GovTribe. Require the actual book/version and coefficient rules.
- Cooperative construction/facilities pricing may have local participation limits and adders.

## Failure modes

- Pricing construction from award totals or square-foot averages without scope normalization.
- Treating A-E as construction or using low bid as its universal model.
- Inventing quantities, productivity, quotes, bond rates, or site conditions.
- Comparing state/local line items with different trade units or bundled scope.
- Omitting general conditions, phasing, safety, quality, closeout, cash flow, or escalation.
- Treating facilities O&M staffing evidence as new-construction cost.
