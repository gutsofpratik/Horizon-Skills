# Industry-Aware Pricing: Digital, IT, Cyber, and Data

Use alongside the matching pricing workflow after the economic model clearly resolves to this primary lane.

## Resolve the subtype and price unit

### SaaS/licensed software

Potential model:

`platform/base subscription`
`+ users/devices/sites/tenants/features`
`+ environments and data/transaction tiers`
`+ implementation/configuration/migration/integration`
`+ training/adoption`
`+ support/service tier`
`+ security/accessibility/compliance effort`
`+ third-party licenses/data/APIs`
`+ options/escalation/renewal`

Key drivers:

- named vs concurrent users, active vs entitled users, device/site/tenant, feature/module, storage, transaction, API, term, minimum commitment, true-up, support, sandbox/test environments, data export, and renewal price protection

GovTribe does not provide current commercial SaaS catalogs, discounts, reseller quotes, or product-specific license metrics. Use user/external evidence.

### Cloud/consumption

Potential units:

- compute instance/time, vCPU/GPU, storage, request, database, logging, backup, egress, network, managed service, environment, support plan, and reserved/committed usage

Build low/base/high consumption scenarios using source-backed architecture and volume. Separate cloud provider cost from integrator labor, managed services, security tooling, and fee. Do not estimate cloud cost from a labor-rate comparable.

### Custom software/DevSecOps

Potential model:

- team composition and productive capacity by sprint/month
- backlog/feature or milestone
- fixed-price outcome with explicit assumptions and risk reserve
- labor categories plus environments, tools, licenses, testing, security, and ODCs

Drivers:

- team topology, velocity assumption, backlog quality, technical debt, interfaces, environments, test automation, release cadence, security, data migration, acceptance, key personnel, clearances, and transition

BLS/GSA/SCI may support labor inputs, but none supplies productivity or scope. Keep velocity and effort as modeled/user inputs.

### Modernization/integration/migration

Drivers:

- application/interface/data inventory, discovery, conversion, licensing, test environments, parallel operations, cutover, rollback, data quality, incumbent support, user adoption, and decommissioning

Use milestone and scope scenarios where inventories are incomplete. Unknown legacy complexity should not be hidden in a blended rate.

### Managed IT/help desk/NOC/SOC

Potential units:

- user/device/site/ticket/month, monitored asset, endpoint, alert, shift, service tier, or fixed monthly service

Drivers:

- volumes, arrival patterns, service levels, channels, resolution tiers, staffing, productive hours, tools, licenses, transition, on-call, locations, clearances, and continuous improvement

SCI can challenge federal incumbent FTE/hour footprint. BLS/GSA can support wage/rate context. Neither replaces a workload-to-staffing model.

### Cyber assessment/engineering/operations

Drivers:

- system count, boundary, control set, assessment type, finding volume, tools, cleared labor, coverage hours, incident response, reporting, travel, authorization support, and remediation scope

Do not price “CMMC,” “FedRAMP,” or “NIST compliance” as a generic package. Price the source-defined activities, boundary, evidence, assessment path, and ongoing obligations.

### Data, analytics, AI/ML, and geospatial

Drivers:

- data sources/rights, volume, quality, labeling, pipelines, environments, storage/compute, model training/inference, API/transaction usage, evaluation, human review, monitoring, retraining, privacy, explainability, safety, and integration

Separate one-time data/model development from recurring operations and consumption. Model/token/compute pricing requires current external inputs.

### Network/telecom/IoT/OT

Drivers:

- sites, circuits, bandwidth, devices, installation, construction, permits, CPE, licenses, recurring carrier cost, maintenance, spares, field service, uptime, monitoring, and decommissioning

Treat hardware, field installation, carrier service, software, and managed operations as separate cost pools.

## Source-confirmed inputs to extract

- users/sites/devices/transactions/tickets/data/storage/compute/environment counts
- operating/support hours, service levels, response/restore, uptime, RTO/RPO, credits/remedies
- hosting/boundary/authorization/security/privacy/accessibility and data-rights obligations
- interfaces, migration, incumbent/government responsibilities, transition, cutover, and exit
- labor categories/FTEs, clearances, certifications, locations, key personnel, and shifts
- subscription/license/consumption metrics, third-party products, options, renewal, price protection, and price template

## Use of current tools

- `BLS_Occupational_Wage_Data`: wage baseline for labor roles.
- `Search_GSA_Labor_Rates`: visible ceiling-rate context for comparable labor categories.
- `Search_Service_Contract_Inventory`: federal service-labor footprint for managed IT, help desk, cyber operations, integration, or other labor-heavy scope.
- `Search_Line_Items`: awarded state/local software, hardware, implementation, or service line items only when the unit and bundle are genuinely comparable.

Do not infer commercial license/cloud price from these sources. A state/local line item labeled “software” may bundle licenses, implementation, maintenance, and term; inspect the parent context.

## Scenarios and sensitivities

Test:

- user/device/site/transaction growth
- cloud consumption and egress
- environment count and retention
- support tier and operating hours
- labor mix/productivity and transition
- third-party licenses/data/APIs
- migration/interface uncertainty
- authorization/security schedule and continuous monitoring
- renewal/escalation and price protection
- service credits/availability risk

## Failure modes

- Pricing SaaS as hours without modeling subscription/license units.
- Pricing custom development from GSA ceilings without scope/productivity.
- Treating cloud and third-party consumption as zero.
- Assuming a FedRAMP status or CMMC requirement defines the effort without the actual boundary and source requirements.
- Mixing one-time implementation and recurring service.
- Comparing products or managed services with different included features, service levels, term, or usage.
