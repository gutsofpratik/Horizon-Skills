# Industry-Aware Pricing: Professional and Mission-Support Services

Use alongside the matching pricing workflow after the economic model clearly resolves to this primary lane.

## Labor-model chain

A transparent labor-led model normally follows:

`source workload and service levels`
`-> labor categories/roles and staffing logic`
`-> hours/FTE/productive-hour assumptions`
`-> direct wage or salary`
`-> fringe and other direct labor treatment`
`-> overhead/G&A or approved indirect structure`
`-> escalation`
`-> travel/ODCs/subcontracts`
`-> fee/profit`
`-> contract price`

Use the company's actual accounting treatment when provided. Do not apply a generic multiplicative burden if it would double count fringe, overhead, G&A, or other pools.

## Source-confirmed inputs to extract

- tasks, workload, volumes, service levels, hours, shifts, sites, response times, and surge
- labor categories, qualifications, experience, education, certifications, licenses, clearances, key personnel, and location
- FTEs/hours, productive hours, overtime, leave, training, vacancy, attrition, transition, and phase-in/out assumptions when stated
- wage determination, CBA, compensation evaluation, living/prevailing wage, locality, and escalation instructions when applicable
- government-furnished space/equipment, telework, travel, per diem, materials, systems, and other ODCs
- subcontracting/workshare, similarly situated entities, small-business goals, and teammate price responsibility
- contract type, CLINs, options, ceiling/not-to-exceed, fee, travel caps, and pricing template

## Source use

### BLS wages

Use for occupation/geography wage orientation.

- Map customer labor categories to defensible BLS occupation proxies.
- Record proxy confidence.
- Use percentile/level deliberately; do not equate BLS occupation level with a solicitation labor category automatically.
- Treat fringe, overhead, G&A, fee, clearance premium, credential premium, recruiting premium, and productive hours as separate assumptions.
- BLS is not a wage determination and may not reflect a niche cleared or licensed labor market.

### GSA labor rates

Use for visible Schedule ceiling context.

- Narrow by labor category, vendor, contract, SIN/category, education, experience, worksite, clearance, and rate year.
- Group sibling contract-year rows appropriately.
- Compare value bases after deriving a burdened labor range.
- Do not label the median ceiling as the likely task-order award rate.

### SCI

Use for federal labor-footprint context.

- Prefer prime rows when analyzing total dollars, hours, FTEs, subcontractor count/share, and derived hourly context.
- Use subcontractor rows for workshare/footprint, not per-subcontractor price.
- Treat derived hourly rate as blended source-row context that may include labor mix, fee, scope, and reporting effects.
- Coverage is not universal and fiscal-year data may not map perfectly to the target scope.

### State/local line items

Use when the awarded line item represents a comparable labor category, hourly rate, FTE/month, managed-service unit, case/call/transaction, or deliverable.

- Match qualification, geography, UOM, contract year, overtime, remote/on-site, and parent context.
- Do not average unrelated role titles or service bundles.

## Service-model variants

### Staff augmentation/labor-hour

Model:

- role/category, qualifications, wage, productive hours, burden, bill rate, escalation, overtime, travel, and vacancy/recruiting

Check whether the customer supplies hours/FTEs or the offeror must propose them.

### Outcome-based/managed service

Model:

- workload, arrival/volume, productivity, staffing, technology/automation, fixed management/tools, service-level reserve, transition, and outcome unit

Do not reveal or rely solely on a hidden labor-hour build when the buyer expects an executable service model.

### Advisory/fixed deliverable

Model:

- work breakdown, staff mix, hours, reviews, travel/ODCs, deliverable acceptance, rework, schedule, and fixed-price risk

Use milestone effort and uncertainty scenarios.

### Contact center/case processing/BPO

Model:

- volume, arrival pattern, average handling/processing time, shrinkage/productive time, service level, channels, language, occupancy, QA, training, technology, supervision, and surge

Avoid unsupported operational formulas; show the assumptions used.

### Training/instruction/language

Model:

- course/session/student/day/hour, development vs delivery, instructor qualifications, class size, materials, facilities, travel, translation, platform, testing, and schedule utilization

Separate curriculum development from recurring delivery.

## Productive hours and staffing realism

Show:

- paid hours vs productive/billable hours
- holidays/leave/training/administration
- coverage and shift overlap
- vacancy/attrition/recruiting
- surge and overtime
- management span and non-billable support
- transition and overlap

Do not use 2,080 productive hours by default. Use a disclosed, company-approved assumption or source value.

## Wage, compensation, and locality

- Apply SCA/SCLS, CBA, FAR 52.222-46, state/local prevailing/living wage, or other labor rules only when sourced.
- Match labor classification and place of performance.
- Separate legal minimums from competitive recruiting/retention wages.
- If a wage determination or CBA is missing, identify it as a critical input rather than using BLS as a compliance substitute.

## Scenarios and sensitivities

Test:

- wage/recruiting premium
- productive hours and vacancy
- labor mix/automation/productivity
- transition overlap and incumbent capture
- escalation by year
- overtime/shift differential
- remote/on-site mix and travel
- subcontractor mix/workshare
- service volume and performance risk
- fee/margin floor

## Failure modes

- Calling BLS wage data a fully burdened bill rate.
- Calling a GSA ceiling a winning rate.
- Calling SCI derived hourly context a labor-category rate.
- Using 2,080 productive hours without disclosure.
- Ignoring transition, recruiting, vacancy, overtime, and key-person premiums.
- Applying a wage rule without the controlling source.
- Averaging unrelated categories, seniorities, qualifications, or geographies.
