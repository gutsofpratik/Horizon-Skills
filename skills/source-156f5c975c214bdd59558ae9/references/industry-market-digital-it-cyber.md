# Industry-Aware Market Intelligence: Digital, IT, Cyber, and Data

Use alongside the matching market-intelligence workflow after the market clearly resolves to this primary lane.

## Market segmentation

Separate at least:

- SaaS/platform subscription
- software license and maintenance
- cloud infrastructure/platform/managed cloud
- custom software and digital product delivery
- systems integration and modernization
- managed IT/service desk/network/end-user services
- cyber operations, assessment, engineering, compliance, and incident response
- data/analytics/AI/ML/geospatial platforms and services
- telecom/network/circuit/radio/IoT/OT and hardware-enabled services

A large “IT services” cohort without subtype controls is rarely decision-useful.

## Search architecture

Use combinations of:

- product/platform/vendor names when exact
- mission system, application, environment, domain, data set, or business process
- modernization verbs: migrate, replatform, refactor, integrate, replace, consolidate, operate, sustain, secure
- service model: SaaS, subscription, managed service, service desk, SOC, DevSecOps, agile team, cloud, hosting, licensing
- cyber/data terms: CUI, incident response, zero trust, identity, SIEM, endpoint, vulnerability, data lake, analytics, AI/ML, geospatial
- access/authorization language only when source-relevant: FedRAMP, ATO, NIST, CMMC, FISMA, CJIS, HIPAA, PCI, GovRAMP (formerly StateRAMP), accessibility
- NAICS/PSC, NIGP/UNSPSC, vehicle, contract family, buyer office, and incumbent

Do not search only “AI,” “cloud,” “cyber,” or “modernization.” Tie the concept to a mission, platform, deliverable, or buyer unit.

## Federal market lanes

Analyze:

- vehicle/GWAC/schedule/IDIQ dependence and task-order behavior
- agency CIO/CISO/data offices, mission-program offices, shared services, and component-level buyers
- incumbent platform and transition dependencies
- software/license reseller versus publisher versus integrator roles
- authorization and data-environment constraints only where sourced
- operations/sustainment versus new modernization
- hardware, telecom, cloud, software, and labor components separately when bundled

Market questions:

- Is the buying model a prime integration contract, product/reseller purchase, subscription, labor-category order, managed service, or outcome-based delivery?
- Does the buyer favor one large integrator, multiple-award pools, specialized small businesses, or platform-specific partners?
- What portion of activity is new build, migration, O&M, cyber compliance, license renewal, or infrastructure refresh?
- Which vehicles and parent instruments actually carry relevant orders?
- Are incumbency advantages driven by platform knowledge, cleared staff, data access, authorization, transition cost, or buyer familiarity?

## State and local market lanes

Common structures include:

- statewide IT master contracts, reseller catalogs, and cooperative vehicles
- enterprise resource planning, financial, HR, permitting, licensing, tax, courts, public safety, education, and health/human-services systems
- managed network, telecom, endpoint, service desk, cybersecurity, and cloud contracts
- city/county/school/higher-education platform subscriptions and renewals
- grant-funded broadband, public-safety, cybersecurity, and digital-equity programs
- board/council approvals, technology plans, capital plans, and public meeting records as external context

GovRAMP (formerly StateRAMP), CJIS, local data-residency rules, privacy statutes, accessibility, and insurance requirements vary. Treat them as source-dependent checks, not universal filters.

## Competitor archetypes

- software publisher/SaaS provider
- cloud service provider
- value-added reseller/license solution provider
- global or regional systems integrator
- specialized application implementer
- managed service provider
- cyber product company or cyber services firm
- telecom/carrier/network integrator
- data/AI/geospatial specialist
- incumbent O&M contractor
- small-business niche prime or subcontractor

A vendor may occupy several roles. Identify whether the award appears direct, reseller-led, integrator-led, or channel-based.

## Early signals

Prioritize:

- expiring software, cloud, managed-service, telecom, or O&M term
- product end-of-life/end-of-support or data-center exit
- CIO/CISO/technology roadmap, budget request, modernization strategy, audit finding, inspector-general finding, or corrective action
- RFI, industry day, draft requirements, market research, prototype, pilot, or proof-of-concept
- platform consolidation, shared-service migration, zero-trust initiative, cloud migration, data modernization, or AI governance initiative
- security incident, resilience requirement, statutory deadline, accessibility remediation, or privacy mandate
- hiring of program leadership or establishment of a new digital/cyber/data office, when officially sourced
- grant award for broadband, cyber, education, public safety, health, or digital services

Treat public statements and news as corroboration until tied to procurement evidence.

## Recompete and transition signals

Strong signals include:

- recurring subscription/license renewal
- task-order or O&M expiration while the system remains operational
- bridge action or short extension
- recompete forecast or early notice
- platform migration or successor-program language
- required continuity of operations, help desk, network, SOC, data platform, or mission application
- expiring vehicle or vendor pool used for related work
- acquisition of the incumbent, publisher channel changes, or product support ending

Classify likely successor path:

- same platform renewal
- competitive reseller/license procurement
- recompetition of implementation/O&M
- platform replacement or modernization
- consolidation into an enterprise/shared-service vehicle
- split between product, cloud, cyber, and services
- insourcing or managed-service transition

## Monitor triggers

- buyer plus platform/system/application name
- exact vehicle/IDIQ/task-order family
- renewal, recompete, bridge, transition, migration, modernization, replacement, or end-of-support language
- RFI/draft/industry day for the mission system
- cloud, cyber, data, AI, telecom, or accessibility initiative with buyer context
- official authorization or compliance change that materially affects the market
- award to a publisher, reseller, integrator, or incumbent competitor in the same lane
- state/local board approval, technology plan, grant, or budget line tied to the system

## Common contamination and failure modes

- treating all IT records as one supplier market
- equating software publisher, reseller, and systems integrator roles
- using FedRAMP, CMMC, CJIS, HIPAA, or GovRAMP as universal requirements
- assuming an incumbent owns the software or authorization
- treating a vehicle award as task-order demand
- treating license ceiling/list price as awarded or likely paid price
- counting hardware refresh, telecom, application development, cyber, and staff augmentation together
- using “AI” keyword matches that describe incidental features rather than a real AI requirement
- treating a pilot or prototype as scaled production demand
- assuming a closed RFI will become a solicitation on the stated schedule
