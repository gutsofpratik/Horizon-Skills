# Industry-Aware Market Intelligence Mission-Domain Modifiers

Load one modifier only when it materially changes the primary industry lane. A modifier supplements rather than replaces the primary lane.

Apply a modifier only when it materially changes the market cohort or interpretation. Keep the primary economic lane visible.

## Healthcare, human services, medical, pharmaceutical, and life sciences

Segment by:

- clinical care or staffing
- medical devices, diagnostics, laboratory, pharmacy, PPE, and supplies
- health IT, EHR, interoperability, analytics, and claims
- public health, behavioral health, human services, benefits, and case management
- research, trials, biosurveillance, and laboratory support
- facilities and medical-equipment installation

Market anchors:

- patient/population/program, facility type, care setting, product/device, clinical specialty, laboratory method, benefit program, payer/claims function, or public-health mission
- VA/VHA, DoD health, HHS/CMS/CDC/NIH/FDA and relevant state/local health, Medicaid, human-services, public-health, hospital, and university buyers
- grant-funded versus contracted activity must remain separate

Early signals:

- public-health funding, waiver/program changes, enrollment shifts, outbreaks, facility expansion, laboratory modernization, device replacement, EHR/claims modernization, corrective action, or consent decree

Controls:

- Do not infer HIPAA, BAA, CLIA, FDA status, clinical licensure, controlled-substance handling, accreditation, or patient-data access from industry label.
- Match device/product identifiers and clinical/laboratory use, not generic medical keywords.
- Nonprofit and community providers may be grantees, subrecipients, or contractors; classify the instrument.

## R&D, scientific, engineering, testing, and prototyping

Segment by:

- basic/applied research
- engineering and technical studies
- modeling/simulation
- laboratory or field testing
- test and evaluation
- prototype/rapid capability
- production transition and sustainment
- scientific instruments and facilities

Market anchors:

- technical domain, technology area, program, laboratory, test range, platform, mission problem, technology readiness, deliverable, and transition customer

Early signals:

- BAA/topic/call, SBIR/STTR topic, challenge, prototype notice, consortium/OTA activity, program announcement, test campaign, laboratory investment, technology strategy, or budget line

Controls:

- GovTribe contract-market surfaces do not necessarily represent grants, cooperative agreements, OTAs, or all R&D instruments. Use the correct grants/program tools or external official sources when the question crosses instrument types.
- A research award is not proof of production demand.
- Separate scientific services, prototype hardware, software, testing, and production.
- Treat intellectual-property/data-rights, export, classified, human/animal subject, biosafety, and facility requirements as source-dependent.

## Environmental, energy, utilities, and natural resources

Segment by:

- environmental consulting/compliance
- remediation and hazardous-material work
- water/wastewater/stormwater
- power, gas, utility infrastructure, and grid
- energy efficiency, renewables, storage, and performance contracting
- natural-resource, forestry, land, coastal, and resilience work
- monitoring, laboratory, and field services

Market anchors:

- site/asset/watershed/utility, contaminant, permit/order, regulatory program, facility, resource, project phase, and funding source

Early signals:

- consent decree, permit deadline, violation/corrective action, resilience plan, utility capital plan, rate case, grant/loan award, disaster mitigation, energy audit, condition assessment, or resource-management plan

Controls:

- Separate studies/design from construction/remediation/O&M.
- Do not infer environmental liability, permit status, utility ownership, or regulatory deadline without official evidence.
- Energy performance and utility contracts may use savings, tariff, or financing structures not visible in ordinary award values.

## Transportation, fleet, transit, aviation, maritime, and mobility

Segment by:

- vehicle/equipment purchase and replacement
- fleet maintenance, parts, fuel/charging, and telematics
- transit operations, paratransit, and mobility services
- roadway/bridge/rail infrastructure
- airport/aviation systems and services
- port/maritime assets and operations
- traffic, tolling, fare, and intelligent-transportation systems

Market anchors:

- asset class, route/corridor, fleet type, facility, mode, authority, grant program, project phase, and maintenance cycle

Early signals:

- fleet replacement plan, vehicle age/mileage, federal grant, transportation improvement program, capital plan, route/service expansion, emissions mandate, charging/fueling infrastructure plan, safety directive, asset condition, or major event

Controls:

- Separate vehicle purchase, dealer channel, upfit, charging/fueling, maintenance, software, and infrastructure.
- Federal-aid grants may fund state/local procurement but do not make every procurement a federal contract.
- Do not treat a master vehicle contract as proof of fleet orders.

## Security, public safety, emergency response, and disaster recovery

Segment by:

- physical security, access control, surveillance, and screening
- law-enforcement/fire/EMS equipment and services
- emergency communications, dispatch, radio, and alerting
- cybersecurity when the primary mission is public safety or critical infrastructure
- emergency management, preparedness, continuity, and exercises
- disaster response, debris, shelter, logistics, recovery, and mitigation

Market anchors:

- agency/incident/mission, equipment/system, facility, jurisdiction, grant, interoperability region, hazard, and response phase

Early signals:

- grant award, hazard-mitigation plan, after-action finding, system end-of-life, interoperability initiative, emergency declaration, exercise, major event, consent decree, staffing/coverage gap, or facility upgrade

Controls:

- Separate preparedness vehicles from incident-specific emergency actions.
- Treat disaster spend as event-driven unless recurring preparedness or recovery programs are evident.
- Do not infer CJIS, criminal-justice access, security clearance, weapons authority, emergency preference, or sole-source authority without source evidence.
- Public-safety systems often combine equipment, software, telecom, installation, training, and maintenance; preserve each secondary lane.
