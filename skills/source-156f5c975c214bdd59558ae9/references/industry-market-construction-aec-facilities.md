# Industry-Aware Market Intelligence: Construction, AEC, and Facilities

Use alongside the matching market-intelligence workflow after the market clearly resolves to this primary lane.

## Market segmentation

Always separate:

- architect-engineer/design services
- design-build
- vertical construction
- horizontal/civil/infrastructure construction
- renovation/repair/demolition/remediation
- specialty trade or task-order/JOC/MATOC work
- construction management/program management
- facilities O&M, preventive maintenance, custodial, grounds, and asset management
- energy/utilities/commissioning/performance contracting

A-E, construction, and facilities O&M use different selection models, competitor sets, and lifecycle evidence.

## Search architecture

Use combinations of:

- facility/asset type: hospital, school, laboratory, barracks, road, bridge, water plant, airport, port, utility, data center, courthouse, housing, park
- project type: new construction, renovation, repair, replacement, rehabilitation, demolition, remediation, modernization, expansion
- trade/system: electrical, mechanical, HVAC, roofing, plumbing, fire protection, controls, security, paving, utilities, structural, environmental
- delivery method: design-bid-build, design-build, CM/GC, CMAR, JOC, MATOC, SABER, task order, QBS
- location/site/installation/campus/facility
- program, capital-project name, project number, solicitation/contract number
- NAICS/PSC, NIGP/UNSPSC, set-aside, and magnitude/value band where supported

Codes alone are particularly noisy in built-environment markets. Validate with project description and source documents.

## Federal market lanes

Analyze separately:

- A-E qualifications-based selection under FAR Subpart 36.6
- construction acquisition and contract type under FAR Part 36
- facilities O&M/service contracts under FAR Part 37 and applicable labor clauses
- agency facilities commands and program offices such as USACE, NAVFAC, GSA PBS, VA, and installation-level buyers where records support them
- single-project contracts versus MATOC/JOC/SABER/IDIQ structures
- geographic, bonding, safety, security, and past-project barriers only when evidenced
- design predecessor, construction successor, commissioning, warranty, and O&M conversion

Do not compare A-E shortlist/selection patterns directly with low-bid construction awards.

## State and local market lanes

Common evidence includes:

- capital improvement plans and adopted capital budgets
- bond measures and debt issuance
- transportation improvement programs
- school/facility master plans
- water/wastewater utility plans and consent orders
- airport/port/transit capital programs
- public meeting agendas and governing-board approvals
- RFQ/QBS for design, followed by bid or RFP for construction
- prequalification lists, contractor licensing, local preference, prevailing wage, project labor agreement, DBE/MWBE goals, and bid security where sourced
- JOC/on-call/term contracts for recurring repairs and trades

Use official owner and jurisdiction sources as external context. A capital plan line does not prove solicitation timing.

## Competitor archetypes

- local/regional general contractor
- national general contractor/construction manager
- civil/heavy highway contractor
- specialty trade contractor
- design firm/A-E consultant
- design-build team
- environmental/remediation contractor
- facilities O&M provider
- JOC/MATOC/term-contract holder
- energy service company/utility contractor
- small/disadvantaged/local certified contractor

Normalize joint ventures, mentor-protégé entities, design-build teams, and trade subcontractors carefully. A named designer or subcontractor is not necessarily the prime awardee.

## Early signals

Prioritize:

- adopted capital plan, bond authorization, grant, appropriations, or project-program announcement
- planning, feasibility, environmental review, survey, geotechnical, or design procurement
- A-E award preceding construction
- land/site acquisition, permitting, utility relocation, or owner-furnished equipment procurement
- prequalification, sources sought, industry day, draft RFP, pre-bid notice, or site visit
- capital-project phase completion and next-phase funding
- condition assessment, deferred maintenance, failure, code requirement, resilience mandate, or consent decree
- JOC/MATOC/IDIQ on-ramp, refresh, or ordering-period expiration
- emergency repair, disaster recovery, or hazard-mitigation funding

## Recompete and phase-transition signals

Construction itself is often a one-time project. Classify the successor correctly:

- design to construction
- construction to commissioning/warranty
- construction to facilities O&M
- phase 1 to phase 2 or program lot
- recurring JOC/MATOC/term-contract refresh
- recurring custodial/grounds/HVAC/facilities service recompete
- rehabilitation/replacement after asset lifecycle
- no follow-on procurement visible

Strong facilities-service recompete signals resemble professional services; strong capital-project signals resemble phase progression, not “recompetition.”

## Monitor triggers

- project/facility/asset name and number
- capital-plan status, funding, bond, grant, or board approval
- design RFQ/A-E award
- permitting/environmental milestone
- construction solicitation, prequalification, site visit, addendum, or bid opening
- JOC/MATOC/on-call/term-contract expiration or on-ramp
- facility condition, deferred maintenance, code, resilience, or consent-order language
- award, protest, termination, rebid, bid rejection, or scope split
- commissioning, warranty, O&M, energy, or maintenance successor action

## Common contamination and failure modes

- blending A-E, construction, and facilities O&M
- treating every capital-plan project as a near-term solicitation
- using project magnitude/ceiling as awarded spend
- counting design and construction as duplicate records or, conversely, as the same competitor market
- comparing projects across incompatible facility types, trades, delivery methods, and geographies
- inferring bonding, license, safety, or local certification from an award
- treating a one-time construction award as recurring demand
- missing JVs, design-build teams, and subcontractor roles
- applying federal Davis-Bacon or FAR rules automatically to state/local work
- ignoring federal-aid flowdowns where the solicitation actually includes them
