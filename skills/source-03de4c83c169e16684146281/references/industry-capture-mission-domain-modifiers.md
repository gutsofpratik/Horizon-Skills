# Industry-Aware Capture Mission-Domain Modifiers

Load one modifier only when it materially changes the primary industry lane. A modifier supplements rather than replaces the primary lane.

Apply a modifier only when it changes eligibility, performance, proof, competitive field, or economics. Place modifier checks in the same gate table as the primary lane so the final decision has one coherent record.

## Healthcare, human services, medical, pharmaceutical, and life sciences

**Trigger examples:** clinical services, public health, claims, care management, behavioral health, medical staffing, laboratories, devices, pharmaceuticals, medical logistics, health IT, human services, benefits, or patient/member contact.

**Capture facts and checks:**

- patient/member population, care setting, service area, eligibility, volume, hours, response time, continuity, and outcomes
- professional/clinical licenses, credentialing, privileging, background checks, exclusions, supervision, and scope of practice
- PHI/PII and business-associate obligations, consent, records, interoperability, claims, and breach response when applicable
- facility, laboratory, pharmacy, device, product, cold-chain, controlled-substance, quality, adverse-event, or accreditation requirements when source-triggered
- staffing ratios, on-call coverage, rural access, language/cultural competence, network adequacy, and subcontracted providers
- reimbursement or unit-of-service structure, encounter/claim risk, no-show/utilization assumptions, and continuity of care
- verify FDA status terminology for products and devices; do not call a product “FDA certified” unless that is the precise official status

**Past-performance/competitor emphasis:** same population, care setting, regulated function, geography, volume, data sensitivity, credential mix, and measured health/service outcome. Separate providers, payers/administrators, health IT firms, staffing firms, laboratories, distributors, and device manufacturers.

## R&D, scientific, engineering, testing, and prototyping

**Trigger examples:** basic/applied research, studies, experiments, laboratories, engineering development, test and evaluation, prototyping, pilot programs, demonstrations, or technology maturation.

**Capture facts and checks:**

- research objective, technical hypothesis, maturity/TRL, state of the art, work packages, milestones, decision gates, and acceptance
- key investigators/scientists/engineers, publications, facilities, equipment, data, specimens, test ranges, and specialized methods
- intellectual property, patents, technical data, software, publication review, export control, foreign participation, and government-purpose rights
- human subjects, animal research, biosafety, radiation, controlled substances, environmental, or lab certifications when applicable
- cost share, other transaction/grant terms, cost-reimbursement capability, accounting system, indirect rates, and milestone funding when sourced
- teaming with universities, laboratories, manufacturers, test facilities, and commercialization partners

**Past-performance/competitor emphasis:** scientific domain, maturity transition, facility/method, key personnel, publications/patents, prototype/test outcome, and sponsor mission. Avoid scoring only by contract value when technical novelty dominates.

## Environmental, energy, utilities, and natural resources

**Trigger examples:** remediation, waste, environmental consulting, sampling, water, wastewater, energy conservation, generation, utilities, forestry, conservation, or resilience.

**Capture facts and checks:**

- media/contaminant, site history, permits, sampling/analysis, waste characterization, transport/disposal, chain of custody, and liability allocation
- professional/environmental licenses, laboratory accreditation, hazardous-waste transporter/disposal relationships, and emergency capability
- utility territory, interconnection, tariff, baseline, measurement and verification, energy savings, performance guarantee, financing, and O&M
- environmental review, protected resources, community/stakeholder engagement, consent orders, grant conditions, and reporting
- seasonal/weather/access constraints, long-term monitoring, disposal capacity, and indemnity/insurance

**Past-performance/competitor emphasis:** same contaminant/media, regulatory regime, utility/asset, site complexity, permit, geography, treatment technology, savings guarantee, and closure/outcome.

## Transportation, fleet, transit, aviation, maritime, and mobility

**Trigger examples:** freight, passenger transport, transit operations, fleet management, vehicle maintenance, aviation services, marine services, mobility platforms, traffic systems, charging infrastructure, or logistics operations.

**Capture facts and checks:**

- routes, origins/destinations, schedules, load/passenger profile, service levels, fleet/assets, utilization, downtime, and seasonal/surge demand
- operating authority, licenses, driver/operator credentials, inspections, maintenance, safety, insurance, permits, security, and accessibility
- fuel/energy, tolls, deadhead, repositioning, storage, terminals, ports, airports, charging, and subcontracted carriers
- vehicle/equipment ownership or lease, age, spare ratio, technology, telematics, and replacement cycle
- pricing unit—mile, route, trip, hour, vehicle, passenger, ton, shipment, stop, or availability—and who bears fuel/volume risk

**Past-performance/competitor emphasis:** same mode, route/geography, asset class, scale, safety/service outcomes, operating environment, and authority. Separate carriers, brokers, fleet lessors, maintenance providers, OEM/dealers, and technology integrators.

## Security, public safety, emergency response, and disaster recovery

**Trigger examples:** guarding, law-enforcement support, fire/EMS support, emergency management, disaster debris, incident response, continuity, emergency commodities, or surge services.

**Capture facts and checks:**

- threat/mission, posts/sites, staffing, response time, command structure, dispatch, communications, reporting, use-of-force or equipment rules, and mutual aid
- licenses, background/suitability, training, weapons/vehicle permits, medical credentials, security clearances, and insurance when applicable
- mobilization time, surge, geographic staging, continuity, supply cache, subcontractor network, credentialing, and disaster access
- safety, incident history, quality, chain of custody, evidence handling, public interaction, and sensitive information
- emergency procurement method, local-area or disaster preference, funding/mission assignment, price controls, standby/activation fees, demobilization, and documentation

**Past-performance/competitor emphasis:** same threat/incident type, jurisdiction, response time, scale, credential set, command integration, and outcome. Verify rather than assume any special emergency authority or preference.
