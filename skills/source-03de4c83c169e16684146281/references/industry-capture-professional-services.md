# Industry-Aware Capture: Professional and Mission-Support Services

Use alongside the matching capture workflow after the pursuit clearly resolves to this primary lane.

## Activate this lane when

Labor mix, people, management, service delivery, knowledge, customer interaction, or staffing continuity drives most of the scope and price. Resolve whether the buyer seeks an outcome-based service, deliverable-based project, labor-hour/staff augmentation model, managed operation, or professional judgment.

## Source-confirmed facts to extract

- PWS/SOW tasks, outputs, service levels, QASP/performance measures, deliverables, workload/transaction volumes, hours, shifts, locations, and surge
- labor categories, minimum qualifications, education, certifications, licenses, clearances, experience, key personnel, resumes, letters of commitment, and substitution rules
- staffing plan, FTEs/hours, productive hours, vacancy limits, response time, incumbent capture, transition, phase-in/out, continuity, and cross-training
- place of performance, remote/on-site mix, travel, telework, government workspace/equipment, local labor market, union/CBA, and geographic coverage
- SCLS/SCA wage determinations, professional-employee compensation evaluation, prevailing wage, living wage, paid leave, or local labor requirements only when sourced
- management, governance, quality, risk, security, training, records, background checks, suitability, and reporting
- organizational conflict of interest, procurement integrity, personal-services, inherently governmental, nondisclosure, firewall, and independence concerns
- contract type, fee structure, escalation, uncompensated overtime, overtime authorization, travel/ODCs, subcontracting, workshare, small-business participation, and option periods
- incumbent staff, current vendor, FTE/hour footprint, service performance, customer satisfaction, turnover, and known transition constraints where evidence exists

## Gate table

| Check | Gate posture | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Required credentials/clearances/licenses | Hard if mandatory at award or start | Candidate/team evidence and achievable timing |
| Key-person availability | Hard when named and non-substitutable | Commitment, resume, compensation, conflict check |
| Staffing and labor-market feasibility | Operational hard when the plan is impossible | Recruiting/retention evidence, locality wages, incumbent strategy |
| Transition continuity | Operational/competitive | Mobilization schedule, incumbent access, knowledge-transfer plan |
| Wage/compensation compliance | Hard when applicable; competitive realism otherwise | Current determination/clause, compensation model, CBA if provided |
| OCI/independence | Hard or remediable | Conflict analysis, mitigation, organizational separation |
| Personal-services/inherently governmental risk | Conditional | Source scope and contract structure; do not assume acceptability |
| Workshare/participation | Hard when mandatory | Prime/sub plan, similarly situated status, certification, enforceable commitments |
| Performance capacity | Operational/competitive | Workload model, span of control, tooling, QA, surge |
| Proposal readiness | Increasingly hard near due date | Resumes, references, plans, approvals, price inputs |

Use FAR 52.222-46 or an equivalent compensation-realism concept only when the solicitation incorporates it or clearly evaluates professional compensation. Use SCLS/SCA, state prevailing wage, living wage, or CBA assumptions only when sourced.

## Fit and past-performance dimensions

- same tasks, deliverables, workload, service level, customer interaction, and mission
- same labor mix, credential/clearance profile, shift, locality, and staffing scale
- same contract type and performance-management model
- same transition complexity, incumbent capture, surge, and continuity burden
- same buyer/office or a mission-adjacent customer
- measured outcomes such as backlog reduction, accuracy, response time, retention, audit quality, training completion, case resolution, or customer satisfaction
- prime versus subcontractor role and actual workshare
- recency, dollar/FTE scale, and relevance criteria stated by the buyer

## Competitor and incumbent analysis

Segment the field by operating model:

- incumbent or predecessor workforce operator
- large multi-service integrator
- specialized advisory or technical boutique
- staffing/temporary-help provider
- BPO/contact-center/case-management operator
- training/instruction/language provider
- local or regional service firm
- socioeconomic specialist or similarly situated teammate
- vehicle incumbent with no deep subject-matter advantage

Use SCI, award history, task-order relationships, labor categories, buyer office, geography, set-aside, and contract type where available. SCI can illuminate reported labor footprint and workshare, but its derived hourly rate is not a loaded labor-category rate and its coverage is not complete.

## Workflow adaptations

- **Relevant opportunities:** rank task and labor-model fit, buyer adjacency, credentials, location, transition, staffing scale, and access. A NAICS match alone is insufficient.
- **Bid/no-bid:** the current engine is most compatible with this lane, but still source every clearance, certification, staffing, transition, and key-person assumption. Test solo, teammate, sub-only, and monitor paths.
- **Past performance:** score the top relevant examples on task, buyer, scale, workforce, contract type, role, recency, and outcome; distinguish prime and sub work.
- **Likely bidders:** use incumbent workforce, buyer office, vehicle, labor category, PSC/NAICS, local footprint, and award concentration.
- **Black hat:** evaluate staffing realism, key personnel, technical/management approach, past performance, compensation, transition, price, and small-business participation as the source directs.
- **Incumbent:** use direct award/order linkage and SCI/award context; distinguish the contract holder from subcontractors providing material hours.
- **PTW handoff:** pass labor categories, FTEs/hours, productive-hour assumptions, shifts, locality, clearances, credentials, wage determination, transition, escalation, travel/ODCs, workshare, and contract type.
- **Pipeline:** tag the operating model (`advisory`, `PMO`, `staff_augmentation`, `training`, `contact_center`, `BPO`, `mission_support`) and the primary staffing/transition issue.

## State/local considerations

- Verify jurisdiction-specific professional licensing, background checks, fingerprinting, local wage/living-wage rules, union/CBA requirements, staffing agency licensing, teacher/clinical credentials, data/privacy rules, disadvantaged-business goals, local preferences, and cooperative availability.
- Public-sector staffing contracts may use rate cards or temporary-help catalogs; compare like labor categories, qualifications, geography, overtime, and contract year.
- Do not assume federal labor clauses apply unless incorporated or attached through federal funding.

## Common failure modes

- Treating every service as staff augmentation when the buyer expects outcomes or fixed deliverables.
- Assuming incumbent personnel will transfer at the modeled wage.
- Treating SCI derived hourly context as a bill rate or per-person rate.
- Hiding vacancy, recruiting, credentialing, overtime, transition, or productive-hour assumptions.
- Using generic corporate experience instead of contract-specific relevance.
- Assuming a required certification can be supplied after award when the source requires it at proposal or award.
