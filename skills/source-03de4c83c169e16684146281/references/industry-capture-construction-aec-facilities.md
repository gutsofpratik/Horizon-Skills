# Industry-Aware Capture: Construction, AEC, and Facilities

Use alongside the matching capture workflow after the pursuit clearly resolves to this primary lane.

## Resolve the subtype before capture analysis

- **Construction/public works:** price, responsiveness, bonding, trades, means/methods, schedule, safety, site conditions, and subcontractor coverage dominate.
- **Design-build:** both design qualifications and construction execution matter; phase-one and phase-two procedures may differ.
- **Architect-engineer/licensed design:** qualifications, specialized experience, professional registration, team, location, capacity, and past performance may control selection. Federal A-E acquisition under FAR Subpart 36.6 is qualifications-based and is not an ordinary low-price construction competition.
- **Renovation/repair/remediation:** existing conditions, phasing, occupied-site constraints, hazardous materials, unknowns, and selective demolition dominate.
- **Facilities O&M/MRO:** recurring service levels, asset inventory, preventive/corrective maintenance, staffing, work orders, materials, and transition dominate; apply professional-services pricing logic where labor-led.
- **Energy/utilities/commissioning:** baselines, measurement and verification, savings/performance guarantees, interconnection, equipment, financing, and long-term O&M may dominate.

## Source-confirmed facts to extract

- project type, facility/asset, location, site access, security, occupied conditions, work hours, phasing, shutdowns, outages, escorts, and badging
- drawings, specifications, design criteria, addenda, geotechnical/environmental data, surveys, BIM/GIS/CAD, basis of design, standards, and document precedence
- scope quantities, alternates, allowances, unit prices, bid schedule, magnitude, options, owner-furnished items, salvage, and government property
- procurement method, prequalification, QBS/SF 330, sealed bid, best value, two-phase design-build, technical proposal, oral presentation, or interview
- bonding, bid security, insurance, retainage, payment terms, liquidated damages, warranties, performance guarantees, and financing
- wage determination, certified payroll, apprenticeship, project labor agreement, union, local hire, or prevailing-wage requirements when sourced
- licenses, professional registration, permits, utility coordination, inspections, code, environmental approvals, and authority having jurisdiction
- safety, accident prevention, EM 385-1-1 or other owner standard, quality control, testing, commissioning, submittals, RFIs, daily reporting, closeout, as-builts, and training
- schedule, milestones, notice to proceed, long-lead materials, procurement, weather, production rates, site logistics, mobilization, and demobilization
- subcontractor/trade coverage, self-performance, small-business goals, workshare, specialty certifications, and supplier availability
- site visit/pre-bid conference details, attendance rules, questions, existing conditions, and representations

## Gate table

| Check | Gate posture | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Bonding/bid security | Hard when required | Surety confirmation, aggregate/single-project capacity, form/timing |
| License/registration/prequalification | Hard when required by proposal/award/start | Current jurisdiction and discipline evidence |
| Site access/visit | Hard only if mandatory; otherwise major risk | Attendance record, access plan, site intelligence |
| Trade/subcontractor coverage | Operational hard when critical scope is uncovered | Qualified quotes/commitments, capacity, certifications |
| Schedule/long-lead feasibility | Operational hard when impossible | CPM/procurement logic, supplier dates, phasing and outage plan |
| Wage/payroll compliance | Hard when applicable | Current wage decision/rule, classifications, fringe/payroll plan |
| Safety/quality system | Hard when qualification threshold exists; otherwise competitive | EMR/record, program, personnel, owner-specific plan |
| Existing/unknown conditions | Risk/conditional | Site data, assumptions, exclusions, contingency, RFI strategy |
| Design capability | Hard for A-E/design-build | Licensed disciplines, SF 330/team, design QA, capacity |
| Cash flow/retainage | Operational | Payment schedule, subcontractor terms, working capital, bonds |
| Small-business/trade workshare | Hard when mandatory | NAICS/trade analysis, self-performance, similarly situated/goal plan |

## Fit and past-performance dimensions

- same facility/asset type, trade mix, project delivery method, occupied/secure environment, and site conditions
- similar magnitude, schedule, phasing, shutdown, long-lead, and geographic constraints
- same owner/facilities command or comparable standards
- relevant safety, quality, schedule, change-order, commissioning, and closeout outcomes
- licensed disciplines, key personnel, design/construction roles, and self-performance
- federal A-E: specialized experience, professional qualifications, capacity, location/knowledge, and comparable projects as the announcement states
- design-build: integrated team history and comparable design-construction risk
- facilities O&M: asset count, work-order volume, response time, preventive-maintenance completion, uptime, and transition

## Competitor and incumbent analysis

Separate:

- general contractors and construction managers
- specialty trades
- local/regional contractors
- national programmatic contractors
- A-E primes and discipline-specific designers
- design-build teams and mentor/protégé/JV structures
- facilities O&M incumbents
- energy-service companies and utility partners
- environmental/remediation firms

Search by facility/asset type, geography, owner/facilities command, project delivery method, trade, magnitude, set-aside, contract vehicle, and predecessor project. Local permit, labor, trade, and subcontractor networks often matter more than broad agency revenue.

## Workflow adaptations

- **Relevant opportunities:** rank geography, project type, delivery method, magnitude, bond/license capacity, trade coverage, site/security, schedule, and past-performance fit. Do not use available FTE as the universal delivery measure.
- **Bid/no-bid:** run bonding, license, prequalification, site, design/trade team, schedule, long-lead, wage, safety, workshare, and cash gates before generic scoring. Distinguish construction, A-E, design-build, and O&M.
- **Past performance:** match facility/asset, delivery method, complexity, magnitude, schedule, location, role, and outcome. A generic building project may be weak for a secure laboratory, airfield, utility plant, or occupied hospital.
- **Likely bidders:** use geography, project type, award history, local networks, bonding/magnitude proxies, set-aside, and owner familiarity. Do not claim a vendor's actual bond capacity unless confirmed.
- **Black hat:** reconstruct whether the procurement is responsive low bid, technical best value, two-phase design-build, or QBS. Use the correct model.
- **Incumbent:** distinguish the current O&M contractor, prior construction contractor, designer of record, and IDIQ holder; they may have different advantages and conflicts.
- **PTW handoff:** pass bid schedule, quantities, drawings/specs status, wage decision, trade packages, subcontractor/vendor quotes, equipment, mobilization, bonds, insurance, permits, site conditions, schedule, contingency, escalation, and alternates. GovTribe does not supply takeoffs, federal construction unit prices, cost-book data, or live quotes.
- **Pipeline:** tag `construction`, `specialty_trade`, `renovation`, `design_build`, `architect_engineer`, `facilities_om`, or `energy_project`, plus geography and decisive capacity gate.

## State/local considerations

- Public works rules can include jurisdiction-specific contractor licenses, bid bonds, performance/payment bonds, prevailing wage, certified payroll, apprenticeship, local hire, prequalification, retainage, prompt payment, subcontractor listing, bid opening, and protest requirements.
- Check capital improvement plans, bond programs, utility plans, school/facility plans, and grant funding as early signals, but confirm procurement authority and current project status.
- State/local A-E selection may be QBS or another method; do not assume federal FAR 36.6 applies.
- Local and reciprocal preferences may affect commodities, services, or public works differently. Verify current statutes and solicitation implementation.

## Common failure modes

- Combining A-E and construction into one low-price lane.
- Treating a project magnitude or IDIQ ceiling as expected revenue.
- Assuming bond capacity, licenses, trade commitments, or site attendance.
- Pricing from award totals without scope, quantities, facility type, geography, and date normalization.
- Ignoring retainage, cash flow, escalation, long-lead procurement, occupied-site productivity, or subcontractor exclusions.
- Treating facilities O&M as new construction or vice versa.
