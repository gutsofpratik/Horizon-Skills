# Price-to-Win Quality Checks

Use this checklist before returning a PTW review.

## Quality gates

A GovTribe-ready PTW answer should:

- name the opportunity and agency
- identify the pricing/evaluation basis if available
- separate facts from assumptions
- include a PTW range, not only a point estimate
- open with a decision snapshot that makes the recommended target and immediate pricing action clear
- include visible top-down versus bottom-up reconciliation
- explain confidence and data gaps
- cite the strongest GovTribe evidence
- avoid mixing ceiling values with actual spend without labels
- explain why comparables were selected
- connect price strategy to evaluation factors
- recommend concrete next actions
- keep rendered Markdown/PDF evidence tables narrow enough to read
- use concise competitor response notes instead of a wide table when competitor details would wrap heavily

## Confidence language

Use direct confidence language:

- "High confidence because..."
- "Medium confidence because..."
- "Low confidence because..."
- "This is an early PTW hypothesis, not a final target, because..."

Do not hide weak evidence behind confident language.

## Refusal and redirection boundaries

Refuse or redirect requests to:

- use leaked source-selection information
- use non-public competitor pricing without rights
- coordinate bids or prices with competitors
- submit intentionally unrealistic or non-compliant pricing
- evade procurement rules, socioeconomic eligibility rules, or subcontracting requirements
- fabricate citations, rates, or comparable awards

Safe redirection examples:

- "I can use public award history, GovTribe records, your own cost data, and properly obtained FOIA/debrief data."
- "I can show how your proposed price compares to historical market signals, but I cannot help coordinate pricing with competitors."
- "I can flag below-cost risk, but final bid pricing should be validated by your pricing, contracts, and compliance teams."

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Treating the lowest historical award as the PTW without scope normalization
- Treating IDIQ ceiling value as expected revenue
- Ignoring option years and escalation
- Ignoring whether the procurement is LPTA versus best value tradeoff
- Ignoring set-aside status and vehicle eligibility
- Comparing labor rates across locations without adjustment
- Overweighting a single stale award
- Failing to account for incumbent transition advantage
- Recommending a premium without tying it to evaluation factors
- Producing a generic market overview instead of a company-actionable target
- Forcing every comparable, competitor, rate, and normalization detail into one wide portrait table instead of using companion CSV/JSON artifacts
- Hiding the market/execution gap instead of making it a decision point
- Letting scenario tables carry paragraph-length prose instead of concise pricing posture guidance
