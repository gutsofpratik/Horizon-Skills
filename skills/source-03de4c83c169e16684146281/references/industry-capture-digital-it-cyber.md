# Industry-Aware Capture: Digital, IT, Cyber, and Data

Use alongside the matching capture workflow after the pursuit clearly resolves to this primary lane.

## Activate this lane when

The buyer is primarily acquiring a digital product, platform, cloud service, software outcome, technology integration, cyber capability, data/AI service, network, communications system, or managed technology operation. Hardware-only commodity buys remain in the product lane; add this lane when integration, software, authorization, or operations are material.

## Resolve the subtype first

- **SaaS/license:** subscription, term, user/tenant/device/transaction metric, support tier, product roadmap, and renewal economics dominate.
- **Cloud/consumption:** environment boundary, usage drivers, storage, compute, egress, observability, support, and authorization dominate.
- **Custom software/DevSecOps:** backlog, outcomes, team topology, delivery cadence, architecture, testing, security, and data rights dominate.
- **Systems integration/modernization:** legacy discovery, interfaces, migration, platform dependencies, cutover, and organizational change dominate.
- **Managed IT/help desk/NOC:** staffing, service levels, ticket volume, asset/user base, shifts, tools, transition, and incumbent knowledge dominate.
- **Cyber operations/engineering:** data sensitivity, tooling, clearances, incident/monitoring obligations, certifications, and response coverage dominate.
- **Data/AI/geospatial:** data access, rights, quality, model governance, compute, validation, privacy, explainability, and operational integration dominate.
- **Network/telecom/IoT/OT:** sites, circuits, devices, spectrum, field installation, resilience, OEM ecosystem, and cybersecurity dominate.

## Source-confirmed facts to extract

- current environment, incumbent platform, architecture, interfaces, technical debt, user/site/device counts, ticket or transaction volumes, data size, and operating hours
- required outcomes, features, backlog, releases, deliverables, milestones, acceptance, performance standards, and service levels
- hosting model, cloud service model, environment count, tenancy, regions, impact/category language, authorization path, boundary, inherited controls, and agency responsibilities
- information types, CUI/CDI, PII/PHI, tax/criminal-justice/education data, classified information, export controls, retention, records, privacy, and data-location constraints
- incorporated FAR/DFARS/agency/state clauses, NIST standards, CMMC level, incident reporting, assessment, continuous monitoring, vulnerability, software supply-chain, secure-development, SBOM, attestation, encryption, logging, and zero-trust obligations
- FedRAMP or other cloud status language exactly as stated; current Marketplace status must be verified rather than inferred from old marketing material
- accessibility, Section 508 or state accessibility standards, usability, multilingual, and assistive-technology requirements
- data, software, technical-data, patent, license, source-code, escrow, open-source, API, and model/output rights
- transition-in/out, migration, knowledge transfer, incumbent cooperation, data export, portability, disposal, and exit obligations
- staffing, key personnel, labor categories, clearances, certifications, on-call/shift coverage, location, and surge
- subscription/consumption metrics, minimum commitments, true-up, renewal, options, price protection, cloud credits, third-party licenses, and support tiers

## Gate table

| Check | Gate posture | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| Vehicle/platform access | Hard when procurement or architecture requires it | Vehicle eligibility, platform/OEM relationship, contract path |
| Cloud authorization status | Hard only to the extent the source requires a status by a stated date | Current official Marketplace/status evidence, boundary match, agency-use analysis |
| CUI/CDI/CMMC/DFARS posture | Hard when incorporated and applicable | Clause mapping, current assessment/certification evidence, system boundary and flowdowns |
| Classified/facility/personnel access | Hard when required for performance | Facility and personnel clearance plan, sponsorship/timing |
| Data rights/licensing | Hard when no lawful or economically viable rights path exists | License terms, IP/data-rights analysis, third-party dependencies |
| Legacy/interface access | Conditional and schedule-critical | Interface inventory, incumbent/government responsibilities, data availability |
| Transition/cutover | Operational hard gate when continuity cannot be achieved | Mobilization plan, staffing, tooling, migration/cutover evidence |
| Service-level scale | Operational/competitive | Volumes, staffing/tool capacity, reference performance, reliability architecture |
| Accessibility/privacy | Hard when mandatory; otherwise evaluation/acceptance risk | Conformance evidence, testing approach, privacy/security artifacts |
| Software supply chain | Conditional | SBOM/attestation/process evidence when source-triggered |

Do not equate a FedRAMP listing with an agency ATO, authorization reuse decision, or exact boundary fit. Do not assume a NIST publication applies contractually without a source trigger. Verify the current clause version and program terminology.

## Fit and past-performance dimensions

- same platform, cloud, language, architecture, toolchain, OEM, network, device, or data domain
- same authorization/security boundary, data sensitivity, impact, incident obligations, or cleared environment
- same user/site/device/transaction/ticket scale and operating hours
- same mission workflow and integration complexity
- transition from a comparable incumbent/legacy environment
- comparable DevSecOps cadence, migration, uptime, response, defect, security, adoption, or operational outcomes
- role as product owner, SaaS provider, cloud service provider, prime integrator, MSP, OEM partner, cyber operator, data provider, or staff-augmentation vendor
- relevant data/software rights and delivery model

A generic “IT services” reference is weak when the target is a specific platform, authorization boundary, mission workflow, or high-availability operation.

## Competitor and incumbent analysis

Build separate archetypes rather than one broad IT list:

- product/SaaS OEM and channel partners
- hyperscaler and cloud service partners
- platform-specific integrators
- incumbent operations or service-desk provider
- software development and modernization firms
- cyber specialists/MSSPs
- telecom/network carriers and field integrators
- data/AI/geospatial specialists
- large vehicle incumbents and small-business niche providers

Search exact platform, contract number, vehicle, program, authorization/product name, buyer office, relevant technology, and predecessor awards before broad NAICS/PSC. Account for switching costs, data portability, embedded tools, incumbent staff, agency-owned licenses, and recompete packaging.

## Workflow adaptations

- **Relevant opportunities:** resolve subtype and technology/environment fit; penalize broad keyword-only matches. Rank authorization, platform, transition, data, vehicle, and operating-scale fit.
- **Bid/no-bid:** separate present eligibility from a remediable authorization or staffing path. Make boundary, data, rights, integration, transition, and service-level assumptions explicit.
- **Past performance:** prioritize same environment, security posture, mission process, scale, and measured outcome.
- **Likely bidders:** combine buyer/vehicle incumbents with platform/OEM and specialist ecosystems. A vendor's general IT revenue does not prove likelihood.
- **Black hat:** mirror demos, technical challenges, oral presentations, staffing, past performance, price, and risk factors actually stated. Evaluate incumbent lock-in and migration risk without assuming the buyer will reward disruption.
- **Incumbent:** distinguish platform owner, license reseller, cloud host, application maintainer, infrastructure operator, and service-desk incumbent; they may be different firms.
- **PTW handoff:** pass subtype, users/sites/devices/transactions, environment count, support hours, staffing, subscription/consumption units, third-party licenses, migration, authorization, cloud usage, transition, and service levels. Current GovTribe pricing tools do not supply commercial cloud calculators or SaaS catalog pricing.
- **Pipeline:** tag the exact digital operating model and current authorization/transition unknown, not simply `IT`.

## State/local considerations

- Verify state or local security frameworks, cloud terms, data residency, privacy, records retention, breach notification, accessibility, criminal-justice/health/education data rules, mandatory statewide contracts, cooperative vehicles, reseller requirements, and local hosting/network obligations from the source.
- Do not import federal FedRAMP, CMMC, DFARS, or Section 508 requirements into a state/local procurement unless the solicitation, funding, policy, or incorporated terms do so.
- State/local technology awards may use NIGP/UNSPSC categories and cooperative contracts; category participation does not establish scope or authorization fit.

## Common failure modes

- Treating all IT work as labor-hour professional services.
- Treating an old FedRAMP status, marketing statement, or product-family listing as current proof for the proposed boundary.
- Assuming agency authorization transfers automatically.
- Ignoring third-party licensing, cloud consumption, egress, observability, transition, or exit costs.
- Treating CMMC, NIST, CJIS, HIPAA, or another framework as applicable without a source trigger.
- Comparing a GSA ceiling rate with a SaaS subscription or managed-service unit as though they share a value basis.
