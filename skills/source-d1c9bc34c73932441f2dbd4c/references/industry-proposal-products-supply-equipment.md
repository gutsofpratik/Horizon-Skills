# Industry-Aware Proposal: Products, Supply, and Equipment

Use alongside the matching proposal workflow after the procurement clearly resolves to this primary lane.

## Source package inventory to inspect

- item/CLIN schedule, statement of need, specifications, drawings, standards, approved-products/source lists, salient characteristics, brand-name justification, technical data, and revision history
- quantity/UOM, delivery schedule, destinations, shipping instructions, FOB terms, routing, installation locations, and option quantities
- quality clauses, inspection/acceptance, first article, production lot testing, certificates, calibration, traceability, warranty, and nonconformance processes
- preservation, packaging, packing, marking, labeling, RFID/barcode, hazardous-material, cold-chain, and transportation attachments
- country-of-origin, domestic-content, trade-agreement, prohibited-source, counterfeit, specialty-material, and supply-chain provisions
- bid samples, product literature, technical exception forms, equivalency matrices, manufacturer letters, authorized reseller proof, and substitution/alternate procedures
- pricing spreadsheet, catalog/discount requirements, quantity breaks, freight, installation, maintenance, training, spare parts, and renewal/option lines
- state/local product preferences, cooperative contract terms, dealer/service location, mandatory-contract, recycled/sustainable product, and tax instructions

## Requirement extraction targets

Create discrete source-backed rows for:

- each item identity, model/part/NSN, specification, drawing, revision, and salient characteristic that can independently cause rejection
- every required manufacturer, approved source, reseller, equivalency, or substitute submission
- quantity, UOM, delivery date/location, shipment, installation, and acceptance obligation by CLIN or destination when distinct
- each quality/test/inspection/certificate/traceability artifact
- packaging, marking, hazardous, cold-chain, and transportation obligations
- warranty, service, training, documentation, spares, consumables, and response commitments
- domestic-source, origin, prohibited-source, and supply-chain representations that create a proposal action
- required product data sheets, samples, letters, licenses, catalogs, pricing files, or forms
- evaluation criteria for technical acceptability, delivery, past performance, product demonstration, warranty, or price
- amendment changes to quantities, drawings, revisions, delivery, approved sources, or clauses

Do not collapse a multi-line item schedule into one generic “provide products” row.

## Questions-Risks when not source-confirmed

- Is the proposed item an exact match, approved source, or acceptable equivalent?
- Are current technical data and revisions available?
- Does the supplier/manufacturer relationship permit the required representation?
- Can production, inspection, packaging, and delivery be completed by the stated date?
- Are country-of-origin, small-business nonmanufacturer, or prohibited-source rules triggered?
- Are freight, installation, warranty, field service, consumables, or software licenses included in price?
- Are quote validity and supplier lead time aligned with award timing?

## Annotated-outline architecture

For a narrative product/equipment response, use the source-required structure. Typical evaluated content may include:

1. Compliance/equivalency matrix by salient characteristic.
2. Product configuration and bill-of-material boundary.
3. Manufacturing/source and supply-chain approach.
4. Quality, inspection, traceability, and counterfeit avoidance.
5. Delivery, packaging, transportation, installation, and acceptance.
6. Warranty, maintenance, spares, training, and lifecycle support.
7. Relevant item/platform delivery performance.
8. Exceptions, assumptions, and required product literature.

For a low-price or quote-driven acquisition, avoid creating unnecessary narrative. Focus on exact responsiveness, forms, item eligibility, delivery, and price.

## Evidence and graphics

Use only confirmed evidence. Potential artifacts:

- salient-characteristics compliance table
- item/configuration matrix
- source/authorization letter
- production and delivery schedule
- quality/inspection process diagram
- traceability chain
- packaging/marking visual
- installation/site readiness matrix
- warranty/service coverage map
- past delivery/quality metrics

## Workbook and QA emphasis

- Map each CLIN/item to its technical, delivery, quality, documentation, and price requirements.
- Keep `Each`, `Lot`, `Kit`, `Month`, `Year`, `Hour`, and other units distinct.
- Capture option quantities, minimums, alternates, and destination-specific lines.
- Check that the exact proposed make/model/part appears consistently in technical and pricing volumes.
- Confirm all required product literature, samples, manufacturer letters, and exceptions are packaged correctly.
- Verify that freight, installation, training, warranty, and maintenance assumptions match the price schedule.

## Pricing and capture handoffs

Send Pricing Data:

- exact description/part/model/NSN, quantity, UOM, destination, delivery, source restriction, packaging, inspection, warranty, freight terms, installation/service bundle, and pricing template
- state/local line-item comparables only when UOM, quantity, description, parent award, and timing are comparable

GovTribe does not provide federal product unit pricing, supplier quotes, or live inventory. Keep those as user/external inputs. Escalate unresolved source approval, nonmanufacturer, delivery, or manufacturer authorization to Capture.

## Failure modes

- Treating a catalog page as proof that all solicitation requirements are met.
- Omitting one salient characteristic from an equivalency matrix.
- Using the correct item at the wrong revision, UOM, pack quantity, or destination.
- Failing to reconcile technical model numbers with the price sheet.
- Creating generic packaging/counterfeit requirements not present in the source.
- Calling an award total or incomparable line item a unit-price benchmark.
