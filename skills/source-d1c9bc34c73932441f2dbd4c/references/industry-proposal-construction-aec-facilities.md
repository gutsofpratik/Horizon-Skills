# Industry-Aware Proposal: Construction, AEC, and Facilities

Use alongside the matching proposal workflow after the procurement clearly resolves to this primary lane.

## Resolve the procurement model before extraction

- **Construction/sealed bid:** responsiveness, bid schedule, bonds, forms, acknowledgments, licenses, subcontractor listing, and price can be decisive.
- **Construction/best value:** technical approach, schedule, key personnel, past performance, safety, quality, and price may be traded off.
- **Two-phase design-build:** phase-one qualifications and phase-two technical/price response may have different sources and controls.
- **Federal A-E:** FAR Subpart 36.6 uses qualifications-based selection and negotiation at a fair and reasonable price. Follow SF 330 and announcement requirements rather than a generic construction proposal model.
- **State/local A-E:** verify the jurisdiction's actual selection method; do not assume federal QBS.
- **Facilities O&M:** often labor/service led with asset, work-order, materials, and performance requirements; use the professional-services model plus built-environment checks.

## Source package inventory to inspect

- drawings, specifications, addenda, design criteria, geotechnical/environmental reports, surveys, schedules, BIM/CAD/GIS requirements, basis of design, and document-order-of-precedence provisions
- bid schedule, quantities, alternates, allowances, unit prices, owner-furnished items, options, and magnitude
- site visit/pre-bid meeting, questions, access, badging, security, work hours, occupied conditions, phasing, shutdowns/outages, and site logistics
- bid bond/security, performance/payment bonds, insurance, retainage, payment, liquidated damages, warranties, guarantees, and financing
- wage determination, certified payroll, apprenticeship, PLA/union, local hire, and labor classifications when sourced
- contractor license, professional registration, prequalification, permits, authority having jurisdiction, utility coordination, and inspections
- safety/accident prevention, EM 385-1-1 or owner standard, QC, testing, commissioning, submittals, RFIs, daily reports, closeout, as-builts, O&M manuals, and training
- schedule milestones, notice to proceed, long-lead items, procurement, weather, cure periods, commissioning, and turnover
- subcontractor/trade listing, self-performance, small-business participation, workshare, specialty certifications, and letters
- SF 330, A-E evaluation criteria, interviews, design team, specialized experience, capacity, location, and sample projects
- facilities O&M asset inventory, work orders, preventive maintenance, response times, staffing, CMMS, material handling, backlog, and transition

## Requirement extraction targets

Create discrete rows for:

- every bid/proposal form, bond, signature, acknowledgment, license, prequalification, and portal submission control
- each drawing/specification/addendum acknowledgment and any required technical exception or substitution process
- site visit, access, schedule, phasing, outage, shutdown, security, and occupied-site obligation
- wage/payroll, labor classification, apprenticeship, union/PLA, and local-hire obligations when sourced
- safety, quality, testing, commissioning, submittal, RFI, reporting, closeout, as-built, and warranty deliverables
- each key milestone, schedule narrative, CPM, long-lead, and procurement-plan requirement
- each technical approach, means/methods, logistics, environmental, temporary control, and risk response instruction
- each subcontractor/trade, self-performance, small-business, and team documentation requirement
- each A-E SF 330 section, evaluation criterion, page/format instruction, team role, project example, license, and interview requirement
- each facilities O&M asset, service level, work-order, response, staffing, CMMS, material, and transition requirement
- each pricing schedule line, unit, allowance, alternate, option, and attachment

## Questions-Risks when not source-confirmed

- Is the site visit mandatory, and did the team attend under the required identity?
- Are drawings/specifications complete and consistent, and what is the order of precedence?
- What existing conditions, hazardous materials, utilities, access, outages, or owner responsibilities remain unknown?
- Are required bonds, licenses, prequalification, professional registrations, insurance, and trade partners available by the source deadline?
- Which wage determination/classifications, payroll, union, apprenticeship, and local-hire rules apply?
- Are subcontractor quotes complete, current, scope-aligned, and free of material exclusions?
- Can long-lead procurement and phasing meet the schedule?
- Does the project require EM 385-1-1, a site-specific plan, named safety personnel, or another owner standard?
- Is the acquisition A-E/QBS, design-build, construction, or O&M, and is the response using the correct evaluation model?

## Annotated-outline architecture

### Construction/best value

1. Project understanding and critical constraints.
2. Technical approach/means and methods.
3. Site logistics, phasing, occupied operations, and temporary controls.
4. Schedule, milestones, long-lead procurement, and recovery.
5. Safety and accident prevention.
6. Quality control, testing, commissioning, and closeout.
7. Key personnel, organization, subcontractors/trades, and small-business plan.
8. Risk, change management, environmental, and stakeholder coordination.
9. Relevant projects and measured safety/schedule/quality outcomes.

### Design-build

Add design management, design schedule, discipline integration, design reviews, constructability, BIM, permitting, design QA, designer-of-record roles, and design-to-budget controls.

### Architect-engineer

Follow the announcement/SF 330 structure exactly. Typical evaluator questions include specialized experience, professional qualifications, team organization, past performance, capacity, location/knowledge, design quality, and discipline integration. Do not add a price narrative if price is not requested at that stage.

### Facilities O&M

Use asset understanding, preventive/corrective maintenance, work control/CMMS, staffing, service levels, materials/spares, transition, quality, safety, energy, backlog, and continuous improvement.

### Sealed bid

Do not manufacture a technical volume. Build a responsiveness checklist covering forms, bonds, acknowledgment, bid schedule, alternates, licenses, subcontractor listing, representations, and delivery method.

## Workbook and QA emphasis

- Treat every addendum as a potential change to drawings, quantities, bid forms, wage decisions, schedule, or deadline.
- Confirm all bid/proposal forms, bonds, signatures, notarization, acknowledgments, licenses, and portal steps.
- Reconcile bid schedule units, alternates, allowances, options, technical approach, schedule, and subcontractor scopes.
- Verify site-visit identity and attendance requirements.
- Keep A-E qualifications and construction price logic separate.
- Map each SF 330 requirement to the exact form section and page limit.
- Track drawing/spec conflicts and missing reports in `Questions-Risks`, not as resolved assumptions.
- Verify safety, quality, commissioning, closeout, and named-person requirements against the source.

## Pricing and capture handoffs

Send Pricing Data:

- bid schedule/units, drawings/spec status, wage decision, quantities, trade packages, material/equipment assumptions, subcontractor/vendor quotes, mobilization, bonds, insurance, permits, site conditions, schedule, escalation, contingency, allowances, alternates, options, retainage, and payment terms

GovTribe does not provide construction takeoffs, federal construction unit prices, commercial cost books, live subcontractor/material quotes, or current bond rates. State/local line-item evidence is useful only when unit, quantity, project type, geography, parent context, and date are comparable. Escalate bond, license, team, site, schedule, safety, or workshare blockers to Capture.

## State/local considerations

- Extract current public-works forms, contractor licenses, bidder prequalification, bid security, performance/payment bonds, subcontractor listing, prevailing wage, certified payroll, apprenticeship, local hire, retainage, prompt payment, bid opening, protest, local preference, and federally funded requirements.
- Verify whether design services use QBS, best value, or another process.
- Do not assume federal FAR 36, Davis-Bacon, SF 330, or EM 385-1-1 applies unless the source makes it applicable.

## Failure modes

- Using a generic construction outline for a sealed bid or A-E qualifications submission.
- Missing an addendum acknowledgment, bond, signature, license, or bid-schedule line.
- Treating a site condition or drawing conflict as resolved without a source or RFI.
- Ignoring wage/payroll, long-lead, trade, phasing, and closeout dependencies.
- Presenting unsourced bond capacity, safety metrics, or subcontractor commitments.
- Letting technical, schedule, and pricing assumptions diverge.
