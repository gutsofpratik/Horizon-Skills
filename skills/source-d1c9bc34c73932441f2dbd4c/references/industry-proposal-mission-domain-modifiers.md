# Industry-Aware Proposal Mission-Domain Modifiers

Load one modifier only when it materially changes the primary procurement lane. A modifier supplements rather than replaces the primary lane.

Place source-confirmed modifier requirements in the normal matrix and outline. Place unsourced checks in `Questions-Risks` or `open_questions`.

## Healthcare, human services, medical, pharmaceutical, and life sciences

### Source areas and requirements to inspect

- population, eligibility, care/service setting, locations, volumes, hours, access, network, continuity, outcomes, and reporting
- professional/clinical licenses, credentialing, privileging, exclusions, supervision, staffing ratios, background checks, and key personnel
- HIPAA/PHI, business-associate terms, privacy, consent, records, interoperability, claims, incident/breach, and data exchange
- laboratory, pharmacy, device, product, cold-chain, controlled-substance, quality-system, adverse-event, accreditation, or FDA/CMS requirements when incorporated
- patient safety, infection control, emergency coverage, language access, accessibility, cultural competence, and service continuity
- unit-of-service, encounter, case, member, claim, specimen, test, bed/day, device, or FTE pricing instructions

### Outline/evidence emphasis

Use care/service model, clinical governance, credentialing, staffing/coverage, patient safety, quality/outcomes, data/privacy, continuity, network/provider management, and relevant population/setting evidence. Verify official product/device terminology; do not call a device “FDA certified” merely because it is registered or listed.

## R&D, scientific, engineering, testing, and prototyping

### Source areas and requirements to inspect

- objectives, hypotheses, technical challenges, maturity/TRL, work packages, milestones, decision gates, deliverables, demonstrations, and acceptance
- key investigators, researchers, engineers, publications, patents, facilities, equipment, methods, specimens, test ranges, and data
- intellectual property, patents, technical data, software, publication review, export control, foreign participation, security, and government rights
- human subjects, animal research, biosafety, radiation, controlled substances, environmental, laboratory, and quality requirements when sourced
- cost share, cost-reimbursement, milestone payment, accounting, indirect rates, grants/other transaction terms, and commercialization

### Outline/evidence emphasis

Use technical hypothesis, state of the art, research plan, methods, milestones, risk/alternative paths, team/facilities, test/validation, data management, IP/rights, transition, and impact. Do not force uncertain R&D into false production-level specificity; distinguish objective, method, milestone, and learning outcome as the source does.

## Environmental, energy, utilities, and natural resources

### Source areas and requirements to inspect

- site/media/contaminant, sampling, analysis, chain of custody, waste characterization, transport/disposal, permits, reporting, and liability
- environmental review, protected resources, community/stakeholder engagement, consent orders, health/safety, emergency response, and closure
- utility territory, baseline, tariffs, interconnection, equipment, commissioning, measurement and verification, savings/performance guarantee, financing, and O&M
- licenses, professional qualifications, laboratory accreditation, transporter/disposal authorization, and subcontractor facilities
- seasonal/weather/access, long-term monitoring, data quality, and regulatory acceptance

### Outline/evidence emphasis

Use conceptual site/asset model, regulatory path, field/lab methods, quality, health/safety, waste chain, schedule, stakeholder coordination, M&V, savings guarantee, commissioning, and comparable outcomes.

## Transportation, fleet, transit, aviation, maritime, and mobility

### Source areas and requirements to inspect

- routes, schedules, origins/destinations, passenger/load profiles, service levels, fleet/assets, terminals/sites, utilization, downtime, and surge
- licenses, operating authority, driver/operator credentials, inspections, maintenance, safety, insurance, accessibility, security, and permits
- fuel/energy, tolls, deadhead, storage, charging, repositioning, subcontracted carriers, and dispatch
- unit of price—mile, trip, route, vehicle, passenger, ton, shipment, stop, hour, or availability—and volume/fuel adjustment rules
- technology, telematics, ticketing, data, integration, incident response, and customer service

### Outline/evidence emphasis

Use operating plan, fleet/asset plan, staffing/credentials, safety, maintenance, dispatch, route/service reliability, surge/continuity, accessibility, technology, fuel/energy risk, and same-mode outcomes.

## Security, public safety, emergency response, and disaster recovery

### Source areas and requirements to inspect

- mission/threat, posts/sites, staffing, response time, command, dispatch, communications, reporting, equipment, evidence/chain of custody, and public interaction
- licenses, background/suitability, training, weapons/vehicle permits, medical credentials, clearances, insurance, and use-of-force rules when applicable
- emergency mobilization, staging, surge, credentialing, mutual aid, continuity, supply cache, activation/demobilization, and documentation
- emergency procurement authority, local-area/disaster preference, funding, price/standby structure, debris/quantity documentation, and reimbursement support

### Outline/evidence emphasis

Use concept of operations, command/integration, staffing/coverage, mobilization, credentials, training, communications, safety, quality, incident reporting, continuity, demobilization, and same-incident/jurisdiction proof. Do not assume emergency rules or preferences without source language.
