# Industry-Aware Proposal: Professional and Mission-Support Services

Use alongside the matching proposal workflow after the procurement clearly resolves to this primary lane.

## Resolve the service model

- outcome/performance-based managed service
- fixed-price deliverable/project
- labor-hour/time-and-materials support
- staff augmentation/temporary staffing
- advisory/consulting/professional judgment
- BPO/contact center/case management
- training/instruction/language
- mixed service with products, travel, or field operations

The proposal should explain the promised outcome and management system, not merely list people, unless the source is explicitly labor-category driven.

## Source package inventory to inspect

- PWS/SOW/SOO tasks, deliverables, workload/transaction data, QASP/surveillance, service levels, performance measures, reports, and acceptance
- Section L/M, staffing tables, labor-category qualifications, key personnel, resumes, commitment letters, interviews/orals, and substitution rules
- transition/phase-in/out, incumbent information, government-furnished space/equipment, staffing assumptions, work hours, shifts, locations, telework, travel, and surge
- wage determinations, collective bargaining agreements, FAR 52.222-46 or equivalent compensation-realism instructions, living wage, paid leave, overtime, union, and locality rules when incorporated
- clearances, suitability, background checks, licenses, certifications, training, access, and onboarding
- management, governance, quality, risk, continuity, recruiting, retention, vacancy, span of control, escalation, and customer-service requirements
- OCI, independence, procurement integrity, personal-services, inherently governmental, nondisclosure, firewall, and conflict provisions
- subcontracting plan, small-business participation, workshare, similarly situated entities, teaming letters, and consent requirements
- pricing labor categories, hours/FTEs, productive hours, escalation, travel/ODCs, fee, options, and basis-of-estimate instructions

## Requirement extraction targets

Create discrete rows for:

- each PWS task, deliverable, workload, service level, standard, and report that requires a response or acceptance
- each labor category, qualification, certification, license, clearance, location, schedule, and coverage requirement
- each key-person resume/letter/interview/substitution requirement
- transition milestones, staffing ramp, incumbent coordination, continuity, and phase-out
- management, quality, risk, recruiting, retention, training, surge, and reporting instructions
- sourced wage, compensation, CBA, overtime, or labor-compliance requirements
- OCI/conflict, independence, nondisclosure, personal-services, and organizational-separation submissions
- small-business participation, subcontracting, workshare, letters, and reporting
- past-performance quantity, recency, relevance, role, reference, and questionnaire instructions
- labor-pricing, hours/FTE, escalation, travel, ODC, and price-template requirements

Split workload, staffing, management, and pricing directions when different owners can miss them independently.

## Questions-Risks when not source-confirmed

- What is the current workload, backlog, staffing, vacancy, service performance, and incumbent labor mix?
- Are key personnel available, conflict-free, and willing to sign commitments?
- What local labor market, wage determination, CBA, clearance, credential, and retention assumptions apply?
- Will incumbent personnel be accessible, and what capture/retention rate is realistic?
- What productive-hour, overtime, leave, training, vacancy, and surge assumptions drive price?
- Does the work risk personal-services or inherently governmental performance, and what management boundaries are needed?
- Is there an OCI, independence, or firewall issue?
- What prime/sub workshare and small-business commitments are required and supportable?

## Annotated-outline architecture

Typical evaluated modules:

1. Understanding of mission, workload, users, and service outcomes.
2. Technical/service-delivery approach by task or process.
3. Organization, governance, decision rights, and customer interface.
4. Staffing model, labor mix, key personnel, recruiting, retention, and coverage.
5. Transition, incumbent capture, knowledge transfer, and continuity.
6. Quality, performance management, QASP alignment, metrics, and continuous improvement.
7. Risk, surge, continuity, security, and issue escalation.
8. Training, workforce development, and knowledge management.
9. Small-business/team integration and workshare.
10. Relevant past performance and measurable outcomes.

For staff augmentation, prioritize qualification mapping, availability, recruiting pipeline, retention, timekeeping, supervision boundaries, and rate assumptions. For outcome-based services, prioritize process, capacity, controls, automation, service levels, and results.

## Evidence and graphics

Potential artifacts:

- service-delivery process map
- organization/governance chart
- staffing by task/shift/site
- labor-category qualification matrix
- recruiting/credentialing funnel
- transition schedule and day-one readiness matrix
- span-of-control and escalation model
- QASP/performance metric crosswalk
- quality and corrective-action cycle
- workload/capacity model
- small-business workshare matrix
- past-performance relevance table and outcome metrics

## Workbook and QA emphasis

- Connect every Section M staffing, management, transition, and past-performance criterion to explicit outline content and evidence.
- Reconcile labor categories, qualifications, FTEs/hours, shifts, locations, key personnel, and price tables.
- Confirm every resume contains the required elements and maps to the correct labor category/key role.
- Preserve exact wage determination, CBA, clause, and effective-date information when relevant.
- Verify commitment letters, certifications, clearances, licenses, and teammate evidence are current and appropriately worded.
- Track unresolved incumbent staffing and productive-hour assumptions in `Questions-Risks` and the pricing handoff.
- Do not use generic “best practices” as proof; require named processes, owners, metrics, and evidence.

## Pricing and capture handoffs

Send Pricing Data:

- labor categories, minimum qualifications, FTEs/hours, productive hours, shifts, location/remote mix, clearances, certifications, wage decision/CBA, escalation, transition, overtime, travel/ODCs, workshare, contract type, options, and price template

Use BLS as wage evidence, GSA rates as visible Schedule ceiling context, SCI as federal labor-footprint context, and state/local line items only when comparable. Do not mix their semantics. Escalate unavailable key staff, impossible recruiting, transition, access, OCI, or workshare gaps to Capture.

## State/local considerations

- Extract jurisdiction-specific professional licenses, background/fingerprinting, staffing-agency rules, living/prevailing wage, union/CBA, teacher/clinical credentials, local hiring, disadvantaged-business goals, public-records/confidentiality, and required affidavits.
- Compare state/local rate cards only when labor category, qualifications, geography, overtime, contract year, and pricing basis match.
- Do not import federal SCLS or FAR 52.222-46 unless incorporated or otherwise controlling.

## Failure modes

- Turning an outcome-based service into a resume catalog.
- Hiding staffing and price assumptions in narrative prose.
- Assuming incumbent capture without a sourced or user-confirmed plan.
- Treating SCI derived hourly context as a bill rate.
- Failing to reconcile resumes, staffing tables, workshare, and price volume.
- Creating an OCI or compensation requirement from industry practice rather than source text.
