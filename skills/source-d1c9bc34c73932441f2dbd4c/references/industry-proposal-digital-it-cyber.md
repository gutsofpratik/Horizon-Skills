# Industry-Aware Proposal: Digital, IT, Cyber, and Data

Use alongside the matching proposal workflow after the procurement clearly resolves to this primary lane.

## Resolve the subtype and system boundary

Before extraction, determine whether the source is acquiring:

- a commercial product/subscription
- a cloud service or environment
- custom development/DevSecOps
- modernization, migration, or systems integration
- managed IT/help desk/NOC/SOC
- cyber assessment/engineering/operations
- data, analytics, AI/ML, or geospatial capability
- network, telecom, IoT/OT, or hardware-plus-installation
- a hybrid of these

Record the customer system, proposed system/service boundary, government-furnished environment, third-party products, data flows, interfaces, users/sites/devices, and operational hours when the source supports them.

## Source package inventory to inspect

- PWS/SOW/SOO, architecture diagrams, interface control documents, data dictionaries, inventories, backlog, technical environment, incumbent tools, transition inventories, and government-furnished property/information
- Section L/M, technical challenge, demonstration, oral presentation, scenario, sample task, usability test, and proof-of-concept instructions
- service levels, performance metrics, ticket/transaction/user/device/site volumes, operating hours, availability, latency, RTO/RPO, incident priorities, and credits/remedies
- security classification guides, CUI/CDI/PII/PHI language, privacy impact materials, incident reporting, continuous monitoring, vulnerability, configuration, logging, encryption, identity, supply-chain, SBOM, secure-development, and assessment requirements
- FedRAMP/cloud authorization wording, impact/category, boundary, agency sponsor/use, Marketplace evidence, package access, continuous-monitoring, and authorization schedule
- DFARS/CMMC/NIST clauses and assessment requirements exactly as incorporated; do not substitute a newer publication automatically for the contract text
- accessibility/Section 508 or state accessibility standards, VPAT/ACR, testing, exceptions, and remediation
- data/software/technical-data rights, licenses, source code, escrow, APIs, open source, model/output rights, records, retention, portability, and transition-out
- staffing, key personnel, labor categories, certifications, clearances, on-call coverage, location, and surge
- pricing metrics, subscription tiers, consumption, cloud credits, third-party licenses, user/device/site counts, implementation, migration, support, options, and renewal

## Requirement extraction targets

Create discrete rows for:

- each technical outcome, interface, migration, data conversion, environment, release, testing, and acceptance obligation
- every service level, performance measure, reporting threshold, credit/remedy, and coverage window that affects solution or price
- each source-confirmed security, privacy, incident, assessment, authorization, accessibility, records, and supply-chain obligation
- each required status, plan, artifact, certification, assessment, report, staffing role, or response time
- every data/software/license/right, deliverable format, repository, transition-out, and portability obligation
- every oral, demo, scenario, challenge, sample, and technical-volume instruction
- each pricing unit, license metric, consumption assumption, environment, option, support tier, and price template instruction
- each evaluation factor/subfactor and its evidence burden

Do not create one broad “comply with cybersecurity” row when the source contains separable obligations.

## Questions-Risks when not source-confirmed

- What is the exact system/service boundary and data classification?
- Is the required cloud service and proposed boundary currently in the needed official status?
- Does the agency expect authorization reuse, a new authorization, an authority-to-use, or another path?
- Which NIST/DFARS/CMMC version and assessment evidence are contractually controlling?
- Who owns interfaces, data extraction, incumbent cooperation, test data, and cutover?
- What user/site/device/ticket/transaction/storage/egress volumes drive price?
- What third-party licenses, tools, cloud services, APIs, models, or data sets are required?
- What rights must the government receive, and can the proposed product/license support them?
- What accessibility, privacy, records, AI governance, or state-specific standards apply?
- Are named/key staff, clearances, certifications, and shift coverage available?

## Annotated-outline architecture

Use the required structure. Common evaluated modules include:

1. Mission/user understanding and outcome model.
2. Solution architecture and boundary.
3. Technical approach by task, feature, or capability.
4. Integration, data, migration, and transition/cutover.
5. Security, privacy, authorization, and supply-chain assurance.
6. DevSecOps/operations, testing, configuration, release, and observability.
7. Service management, help desk/NOC/SOC, SLAs, continuity, and incident response.
8. Accessibility, user experience, adoption, and training.
9. Staffing, key personnel, governance, quality, risk, and metrics.
10. Data/software rights, portability, transition-out, and lifecycle support.
11. Demonstration/oral/scenario package when evaluated.

For SaaS, emphasize product fit, configuration, boundary, implementation, adoption, support, roadmap, data portability, and subscription economics. For custom development, emphasize backlog/outcomes, architecture, team, delivery cadence, testing, security, and rights. For managed operations, emphasize transition, staffing, tools, volumes, service levels, and continuous improvement.

## Evidence and graphics

Potential artifacts, only when supported:

- system context/boundary diagram
- deployment and environment model
- data-flow/interface map
- migration/cutover roadmap
- DevSecOps pipeline and quality gates
- control/requirement traceability matrix
- authorization responsibility matrix
- incident and escalation flow
- SLA/metric dashboard mockup
- staffing/coverage model
- product roadmap and release cadence
- accessibility test evidence
- data rights and transition-out matrix

Do not display proprietary architecture, security details, or customer data beyond what the response requires.

## Workbook and QA emphasis

- Crosswalk every evaluated factor to the technical, management, security, transition, and evidence sections that answer it.
- Track requirement applicability by system component or boundary when the source does so.
- Preserve exact versions/dates of incorporated clauses, standards, forms, and security artifacts.
- Reconcile user/site/device/environment/volume assumptions across technical and pricing sections.
- Verify that product names, authorization status language, hosting regions, impact/category, and support terms are current and consistent.
- Treat demo/oral instructions as submission controls with their own owners, timing, environment, backup plan, and compliance review.
- Capture all required security plans, matrices, attestations, ACR/VPAT, incident contacts, and supply-chain artifacts.

## Pricing and capture handoffs

Send Pricing Data:

- subtype, users/sites/devices/transactions, environments, support hours, labor categories/FTEs, subscription metrics, consumption assumptions, cloud services, third-party licenses, migration, transition, authorization, accessibility, data rights, SLAs, options, and price template

Current GovTribe pricing surfaces can support labor wages/rates and some service footprint; they do not provide commercial cloud calculators, SaaS catalog/discount data, live license quotes, or third-party consumption estimates. Escalate vehicle, authorization, platform partnership, staffing, transition, rights, or boundary gaps to Capture.

## State/local considerations

- Extract the exact state/local cybersecurity, cloud, data residency, privacy, records, accessibility, CJIS/health/education/tax data, breach, hosting, and contract-template terms.
- Verify mandatory statewide contracts, cooperative vehicles, reseller/channel rules, participating addenda, local network/site obligations, and jurisdiction-specific forms.
- Do not add FedRAMP, DFARS, CMMC, or federal Section 508 rows unless the source incorporates them.

## Failure modes

- Treating a FedRAMP Marketplace listing as proof of the exact agency authorization or proposed boundary.
- Using outdated program status terminology without verifying current official sources.
- Adding generic cyber frameworks to the compliance matrix without a source citation.
- Ignoring data/software rights, third-party licenses, egress, transition-out, or portability.
- Writing one generic IT outline for SaaS, custom development, and managed services.
- Letting technical and pricing volumes use different volume, user, environment, or support assumptions.
