# Official correspondence

Draft concise cover letters, submission emails, contracting-officer questions, and related formal communications. Recover the sender, recipient role, purpose, relevant source provision, and intended channel from available context.

## Draft the communication

1. Follow any prescribed subject line, form, or letter template. Otherwise use a specific subject identifying the solicitation or subject matter and purpose.
2. Put the purpose or requested action first. Add only the context needed for the recipient to respond.
3. For a clarification, identify the exact provision and ambiguity, then ask a specific answerable question. Do not request hypothetical scoring assurances or imply that the government has accepted an interpretation.
4. For a cover letter or submission email, identify the actual package and accurately list prepared attachments. Include acceptance language, validity periods, signatures, or representations only when required and supported.
5. Use supplied names and contact details. Do not invent a contracting officer, address, prior conversation, approval, attachment, or signature.
6. Keep a professional, respectful tone. Expand unfamiliar acronyms and preserve necessary technical terms. Avoid flattery, adversarial claims, boilerplate legal language, and unsubstantiated urgency.

## Honor the designated channel

- Check whether the source requires a portal, question form, email address, or another channel. Do not infer that email is an acceptable submission method.
- If the user requests an email draft but the formal channel is a question form, provide the requested draft and identify the channel mismatch separately. Adapt it for the form when requested.
- Keep authoring separate from transmission. Use existing permissions and tool capabilities for any explicitly requested send; do not create a new permission rule in this skill.
- Report an actual submission or receipt only with observed confirmation. A draft attachment list is not a receipt.

## Review

Verify purpose, source references, names, dates, attachments, and the requested action. Preserve review-only intent. Keep missing contact details or unsupported representations in the internal handoff instead of silently filling them.

For email deliverables, prefer accessible text and meaningful link labels; let the actual delivery format govern available structure. For a complete submission package, read `references/submission-readiness.md`.
