# Proposal narratives

Use for proposal sections, RFQ narratives, capability statements, and proposal-related plans. Reuse the established requirement map; do not repeat source retrieval without a material gap.

## Structure the response

1. Identify the exact question, required location, evaluation factor, and evidence available for the section.
2. Preserve buyer headings and mandatory language. Allocate attention to stated factors and thresholds; do not invent weights or assume every procurement rewards additional features.
3. State the approach, explain execution, provide supported evidence, and connect it to the requirement. Use this pattern flexibly; do not invent content to fill a formula.
4. Put required evidence in the requested volume or field. Use cross-references for navigation only when permitted, not to replace required content.

## Adapt to the narrative

| Narrative | Include when requested and supported |
| --- | --- |
| Executive summary | The government need, proposed solution, specific benefits, and strongest relevant proof. Keep each theme consistent with the detailed volumes. |
| Technical approach | Activities, sequencing, responsible roles, outputs, interfaces, quality controls, and handling of relevant risks. Explain execution rather than restating the PWS. |
| Management/staffing | Responsibilities, staffing coverage, qualifications, governance, transition, escalation, and continuity supported by the approved solution. |
| Past performance | Actual performer and role, customer, dates, scope/scale, relevant work, and supported outcomes. Distinguish relevance from an asserted favorable rating. |
| Capability statement | Buyer-relevant services, substantiated experience, verified business/vehicle facts, capacity, and supplied contact information. A generic agency meeting does not require a solicitation. |
| Proposal-related plan | Activities, owners, milestones, dependencies, deliverables, and acceptance or quality controls supported by the requirement and approved approach. |

## Evidence and persuasive writing

- Explain why evidence is relevant: similar scope, scale, operating conditions, users, or technical problem. State limits on comparability where material.
- Attribute subcontractor and individual experience explicitly. Do not turn proposed teaming into a claim that the prime performed the work.
- Keep future methods distinct from completed results. Describe a proposed control as a proposal, not as a proven outcome.
- Use quantified proof only when supported; do not manufacture a percentage, rating, reference customer, or savings estimate.
- Preserve approved prices, service boundaries, and staffing. Route unresolved pricing analysis through the bundled `govtribe-pricing-data` skill, using its own installed root, instead of inventing a number during writing.
- For a writing review, identify the exact weakness and needed evidence or clarification. Supply replacement text only when revision was requested.

## Final check

Confirm requirement coverage, correct volume placement, consistency with the approved solution, and supported attribution. Keep unresolved evidence in the internal handoff. Read `references/submission-readiness.md` only when preparing a complete package or assessing submission readiness.
